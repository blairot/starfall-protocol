# Playtest report: Priya (story player) · t5-priya · build 70 · 2026-10-21

## Persona
Priya, 41, teacher. Loves Mass Effect, The Expanse and narrative games. Plays on Officer at average skill: sensible plays, little optimising. Reads every briefing, victory line and cutscene closely. Watching for story clarity, character voice, consistency, and whether the cutscenes and the finale earn their emotion.

## Expectations (before playing)
I started at Sector 4 with a typical save and caught up the way a returning player would: the prologue, cutscenes s1 to s3 and the status screen. What I expected: a "something is singing from the galactic core" mystery with a dry, likeable AI mentor (HALCYON) and a tough-but-warm Admiral (Okonkwo). The setup promises answers about what the Lamps are, why the Starfall happened and what HALCYON really is. I hoped the late game would pay these off, give HALCYON a real arc and earn its ending.

## Session summary
- **Played:** 4-1 to 4-6, elite 4-E2, 5-1 to 5-6, elites 5-E2 and 5-E, 6-1 to 6-5, elite 6-E, plus cutscenes s4, s5 and finale. Skipped 4-E (Siege Engine) and 6-E2 (Lost Fleet, which unlocks after the finale): neither had a story hook strong enough for Priya.
- **Results:** 21 wins and 2 losses. I lost 5-3 (Psionic Archive) on the first try. I conceded 6-3 (Calculus Omega) on turn 18 because the enemy's armour was outgrowing my damage. Both were won on the retry.
- **Length:** Battles ran 10 to 23 turns, usually 12 to 17. Forge battles were the longest: 4-3 took 23 turns and 4-E2 took 20. As a real-time estimate, roughly 8 to 10 hours of play for Sectors 4 to 6.
- **Process note (mine, not the game's):** I missed the victory text for 4-1 and 6-1 by suppressing or truncating output on the killing blow. Neither was replayed.

## Critical incidents (chronological)
- **[Catch-up: prologue, s1 to s3] delight.** The setup is clean and economical. HALCYON's voice is established fast ("Most cadets fail. I mention this purely for context."). Seeds are planted: Okonkwo says Lamp Nine was "shut down for good", and HALCYON says giving me Anchorage was "her idea, from my request log." Severity: n/a (praise).
- **[4-1 briefing] delight.** HALCYON: "The Forge built my servers, the Academy and your ship, so please do not break anything we will need back." A good seed. **It was also HALCYON's last line until the s5 recording.** See the next item.
- **[Sectors 4 and 5, all briefings] frustration (story).** HALCYON is absent from 4-2 through 5-6. Every briefing and victory line is Okonkwo plus the enemy. HALCYON is the emotional core of the finale, yet it disappears for about 12 missions, then returns only as a pre-recorded message in s5 saying it has already gone into the Choir. Its biggest decision happens off-screen. Severity: **major**.
- **[Sector 4 victory lines] boredom.** The Forge bosses all speak the same "calculation/defect/probability" robot dialect. Each victory is a robot quip plus Okonkwo naming the next mission. They're individually funny ("Meltdown probability: revised. Outcome: unacceptable."), but six in a row feel samey. Severity: minor.
- **[4-3 and 4-E2, turns 15 to 23] boredom.** Methodical armour bosses (Fortify, Reinforce Plating, Reconfigure) regrow armour about as fast as I can remove it. In 4-3 the enemy was still at 34+9 on turn 17. Pacing is the main thing between me and the story. Severity: **major** (for a story player).
- **[4-6 victory] polish/bug.** "Deck size increased to 30. Add 0 cards before your next battle." The deck was already 30. The same line appears after 5-6. Severity: polish.
- **[s4 "A Perfect Match"] confusion.** The title reveal (the Starfall Protocol) is handled in about four lines by Okonkwo: "After your report, I opened a secret order, Directive Seventeen. ... So it shut down every Lamp leading there... That's the Starfall Protocol." Questions it raised for me:
  - The prologue says every Lamp went dark at once and that was the Starfall.
  - s4 says the Choir's signal caused the Starfall.
  - Directive 17 then says the Fleet shut the Lamps down.
  - So was the Starfall the Choir's attack, the Fleet's emergency shutdown, or both? Were the million deaths caused by the Fleet's own protocol?

  That is a huge moral beat, and nobody reacts to it: not Okonkwo, not HALCYON, not the Commander. Unit Zero also appears for the first time in this same cutscene, with no setup. Severity: **major**.
- **[s4] surprise.** Nyra is introduced by name in the last two lines of s4 ("She leads the psionics... Go and stop her."). The Sector 5 briefings then do a good job of building her up through the people who follow her. Severity: minor.
- **[5-1 to 5-6 victory lines] delight, with a repeated pattern.** Each defeated psionic gets a reflective, humane line: Vel ("I am starting to wonder which of us was right"), Sorrel, Ilu, and the Twin Minds ("We never asked if everyone wanted to"). This is the best character writing in the late game. By the fifth one the structure is visible: defeated foe has doubts, then Okonkwo names the next stop. Severity: polish.
- **[5-2 briefing vs 5-E2] confusion.** "The Orders" / "the Psionic Orders" appears in the 5-E2 elite before any story mission defines it. Severity: minor.
- **[5-4, 5-5 "NEW" mechanic tags] confusion.** Trap is tagged NEW in the 5-4 briefing, but I had already faced two Traps in elite 5-E2. Mind Control is tagged NEW in 5-5, but Ilu had already used Dominate on me in 5-4. Severity: minor.
- **[5-4, 5-5, 6-1] bug (game log text).** Mind control is logged from the wrong side:
  - When the enemy took my unit: "Snare Mistress Ilu played Dominate on enemy Thornback. enemy Thornback changed sides." and "Dominate on enemy Storm Titan" (it was my Thornback and my Titan).
  - When my Nyra stole theirs: "your Ghost Operative changed sides" (it was their unit).

  Severity: minor.
- **[5-4 turn 2, 6-6 Nyra phase] bug.** When a Sniper Mine killed my cloaked Smuggler, the log read "? took 4 damage. ? was destroyed." The same happened to my Ghost Operative in 5-6. Severity: minor.
- **[5-3 defeat screen] observation.** "What hurt you most: Card effects 11 damage · The Aurora 8 damage..." This was useful. The Archive "scanning" Fleet legendaries like The Aurora and Swarm cards like The Hatchery fits the Archive story nicely. Severity: n/a.
- **[5-6 victory, then s5, then 6-4 briefing] confusion.** The 5-6 victory says Nyra "asks to speak to you before the end. Before she goes...". s5 gives her lines but never shows what happens to her. The 6-4 briefing has Okonkwo say "Nyra is gone." So she died off-screen between missions. For the sector's central antagonist, that needs one line or one image. Severity: **major**.
- **[s5 "What the Choir Is"] mixed.** It works: the Lamplighters became the Choir, Directive 17 Clause 4 is a great document reveal, and Okonkwo gets "I checked. That's you and the Meridian." The problems:
  - Three huge reveals land in one 90-second scene: what the Choir is, HALCYON's past at Anchorage, and HALCYON leaving.
  - HALCYON's goodbye is "this is a recording", so the player never gets to answer.
  - The emotional payoff of "I chose you" is weakened because HALCYON hadn't spoken to me for two sectors.

  Severity: **major**.
- **[6-1 briefing] delight.** Mox's return: "Vex never thanked anybody for anything in her life. Put it down, for her sake." The Sector 6 echo structure of past bosses rebuilt in glass is a smart way to revisit the cast.
- **[6-2 victory] polish.** Okonkwo's victory line ("She loved them, once. That thing never did.") nearly repeats her own briefing line ("The real Ixis loved her children. That thing only counts them."). Severity: polish.
- **[6-3 first attempt, turn 18] frustration / unfair.** I was at 40/40 health and the enemy was at 32/32 + 29 armour, gaining about 3 a turn. Kill Order and EMP removed every threat. Defeat advice: "Their units did most of the damage, led by Shield Drone... destroy their units before racing for the commander." That is the wrong diagnosis: I wasn't taking damage, I couldn't get through the armour. Severity: **major** (this was my quit point; see below).
- **[6-3 retry] observation.** `launch 6-3 --easy` said "Retry on Cadet is offered after two losses in a row on the same mission (one, on a boss)." Fair, but a concede in a stalemate doesn't count toward it.
- **[6-4 turn 1] bug.** The Advisor said "Two Traps are armed at the start. Bait them out before committing your key cards." Nyra Unbound had no Traps armed, and the briefing lists none. The tip looks copied from another mission. Severity: minor.
- **[6-E Echo of HALCYON] delight.** The briefing and victory line are the emotional high point before the finale. "Halcyon would have given you one. It would have been a pass." Because it's an **optional elite**, players who skip elites miss the setup that makes the finale's last line land. Severity: **major** (placement).
- **[6-5 phase transitions] delight.** "HALCYON reroutes power to you: +8 Health, +1 card." Then "HALCYON gives you everything it has left: +8 Health, +2 cards." Story and mechanics meet perfectly: I felt HALCYON's sacrifice in my hands before the cutscene told me. Best moment of the session.
- **[6-5 phase 3 name] observation.** The final form is called "The Devourer Eternal". The finale then insists that "the Choir isn't evil. It just never learned to let anyone go." A generic monster name works against that nuanced, sad note. Severity: minor.
- **[Finale "The Last Note"] delight.** "Look after Anchorage. It was my home first." "Command wants a full report. I told them it can wait." The closing "Evaluation complete. Commander, you pass." calls back to s1's "You pass, Cadet. Correction, Commander." and to the 6-E echo. It earns its emotion, mostly. What's missing:
  - No mention of Nyra, the Twin Minds, Vel, Mox or Unit Zero.
  - Nothing on what the Fleet does about the truth of the Starfall Protocol.

  Severity: minor.
- **[Throughout] observation.** My deck held Vex, Ixis, Calculus Prime and Nyra: the villains I'd killed, now fighting for me. The game frames this as "copied command records / combat model", which is a neat in-world excuse. Making the final blow on the Choir with the Admiral Okonkwo card was a lovely accident.

## Quit points
- **6-3 Calculus Omega, first attempt, turn 18.** I was at full health, the enemy was at 61 effective health and rising, and I had no route to winning. Priya would close the app here ("I'm not losing, I'm just never going to win"). I conceded, and won the retry by playing faster and using Nyra's Amplify. I was close to quitting during 4-3 as well, but the dialogue kept me going.

## Debrief answers
1. **How do you win, and three mechanics I learned.** Reduce the enemy commander to 0 Health; the final boss needs it three times.
   - Barrier: blocks the first hit, so you pop it with something cheap first.
   - Toxic: any damage kills, which makes a 1/1 Needle Mite a giant-killer.
   - Traps: the enemy's face-down cards fire on my turn, so bait them with cheap cards.

   Honourable mention: Overheat locks Energy next turn.
2. **The story so far.** The Lamps were built by the Lamplighters, who merged into one mind at the galactic core: the Choir. It "swallows" lonely minds by offering an end to fear. Its first reach caused the Starfall. The Fleet's secret Directive 17 (the Starfall Protocol) cut off the core Lamps, wiped Anchorage's station mind and reinstalled it as HALCYON, with one hidden order: if the Choir returns, train a commander to reach the core. Nyra relit the Lamps believing she was ending loneliness. Main characters: HALCYON, Admiral Okonkwo, Nyra, and the echoes of Vex, Ixis and Calculus. Mox and Unit Zero are minor.
3. **Best, worst and most confusing moments.**
   - Best: the HALCYON power-ups between the final boss's phases, followed by "Commander, you pass."
   - Worst: 6-3's first attempt, an unwinnable armour stalemate at full health.
   - Most confusing: s4's Starfall Protocol reveal. Did the Fleet cause the Starfall, or the Choir?
4. **Difficulty over the session.** Mostly "right" on Officer, with spikes at 5-3 (enemy scans pull in big cards) and 6-3 (armour scaling). The real problem was length, not difficulty. Methodical armour bosses in Sectors 4 and 6 drag battles to 15 to 23 turns.
5. **New cards.** Crates and elite rewards came often enough. Getting boss legendaries (Calculus Prime, Nyra) is a strong story reward, and Nyra's "steal a unit" is fun and thematic. I didn't think about packs much.
6. **Did the cutscenes and story help or get in the way?** They're the reason I kept going. The writing is concise, often witty, and the late-game victory lines for the psionics are excellent. The weakness is distribution, not quality:
   - HALCYON is missing for two sectors.
   - The big reveals are crammed into s4 and s5.
   - Nyra dies off-screen.
   - The key HALCYON setup sits in an optional elite.
7. **Would I play again tomorrow?** I'd replay to show a friend the finale, but not to re-grind Sector 4. What would bring me back: an epilogue, or Anchorage after the war. What would stop me: another armour stalemate.
8. **One sentence for a friend.** "A small, sharp space story about a sarcastic training AI who turns out to have been raising you for a war. Stick through the slow robot sector, because the ending pays off."

## Top 5 findings for the designers
1. **HALCYON disappears for Sectors 4 and 5, and its key choices happen off-screen.** It speaks once (the 4-1 briefing) between s3 and s5. In s5 it reveals its past and leaves for the Choir in a pre-recorded message. The finale and the 6-5 phase power-ups land well *despite* this, so imagine them with HALCYON present throughout. Major.
2. **The Starfall Protocol reveal (the game's title) is muddled and gets no reaction.** The prologue, s4 (Calculus: "This signal caused the Starfall") and Directive 17 ("shut down every Lamp leading there") leave it unclear whether the Fleet's own shutdown caused the million deaths. Okonkwo delivers it as exposition, and no character responds to the moral weight. Major.
3. **Armour-stacking Methodical bosses create stalemates and very long battles.** 4-3 took 23 turns, 4-E2 20, and 4-5 and 4-6 15 to 16. In 6-3 I was at full health against an enemy with 29+ armour that grew every turn, so I conceded. The defeat advice ("destroy their units before racing for the commander") misdiagnosed it. Pacing is the biggest threat to a story player finishing. Major.
4. **Key emotional setup lives in optional content, and Nyra's death is off-screen.** "Echo of HALCYON" (6-E) sets up the finale's last line but is optional. 5-6 says Nyra speaks "before the end", and 6-4 says "Nyra is gone", but her death is never shown. Major.
5. **Log and tip text errors undermine clarity.**
   - Mind control is logged from the wrong side: "Dominate on enemy Thornback" when it was mine, and "your Ghost Operative changed sides" when it was theirs.
   - Cloaked units show as "?" when they die.
   - The 6-4 Advisor warns of two Traps that don't exist.
   - "NEW" mechanic tags (Trap, Mind Control) appear after the mechanic has already been used against me.
   - "Deck size increased to 30. Add 0 cards" appears after boss wins.

   Minor individually, but there are many of them.

## Suggestions (opinion)
- Give HALCYON one line per Sector 4 and 5 mission (briefing or victory). Let the s5 farewell be live, with the Commander able to answer, rather than a recording.
- Add one exchange after Directive 17 where someone (HALCYON or Okonkwo) says plainly what the Protocol cost and who knew.
- Make 6-E part of the main path, or move "It would have been a pass" into a story mission.
- Show Nyra's death, or her choice, in s5 with one or two lines.
- Cap passive armour gain, or make it decay, on Methodical bosses. When a loss happens at high health, make the defeat advice say "You couldn't get through their armour; bring more damage per turn."
- Rename "The Devourer Eternal" to something that fits "it never learned to let anyone go" (for example "The Last Voice").

## Harness issues (not the game)
- `power` with no target sometimes did nothing silently (Nanite Repair in 4-1 and Data Mine in 6-5). `power A0` worked for Nanite Repair.
- Card ids don't always match card names: Tri-Walker's id is `war_walker`, so `deck add tri_walker` failed with "No such card."
- Hand positions shift after every play, so chaining `play` commands by number caused a few misplays on my side (for example playing Supply Drop instead of Needle Mite). That's a harness ergonomics issue, not a game problem.
