# Playtest report: Lee (the struggling player) · t2-lee · build 63 · 2026-10-20

## Persona
Lee, 52, coming back to games after years away. Slow to learn rules, makes honest mistakes: plays the biggest card he can afford, hits the enemy commander's face instead of trading, forgets new units can't attack, doesn't count damage or plan a turn ahead. Started on Officer (default). Switches to Cadet if the game suggests it or after repeated losses. Watching for whether the game helps a struggling player: tips, Advisor, Retry on Cadet, defeat screen, whether losses teach anything, and dead ends.

## Expectations (before playing)
"A space war card game. The machine voice says most cadets fail, which makes me a bit nervous. I'm hoping it walks me through things slowly and doesn't punish me for not knowing the rules yet. If it's like those card games my nephew plays, I'll probably get lost by the third fight."

## Session summary
- Played Sector 1 (1-1 to 1-6) and Sector 2 (2-1 to 2-6) in story order on Officer. Skipped all Elites (Lee wouldn't risk an "optional, harder" fight).
- Results: 12 wins, 2 losses. All 11 missions before the final boss were won on the first try. The 2-6 boss (Captain Vex) was lost twice on Officer, then won on the offered "Cadet retry".
- Roughly 14 battles, about 2.5 to 3 hours of real play time.
- Deck building: used `deck autofill` once at graduation. After that I made a few by-feel swaps (took out Supply Drop, Strike Fighter, Combat Stims and Plasma Bolt; put in Harpoon Gun, then Cargo Hauler, Shadow Corsair, Void Plunderer, Broadside and Blast Barricade).
- Commander power: kept Nanite Repair the whole time and never switched.
- At the end: 310 Alloy unspent, and I never found where Anchorage is (see Harness issues).

## Losses (every one)

1. **2-6 Captain Vex, Officer, try 1 (died on Vex's turn 9; Vex was on 2/35).**
   - *Why I think I lost:* At 9 Health I used Okonkwo (7/7) to hit Vex's face instead of clearing her Interceptor and two Det-Bots. On her turn the Det-Bots died attacking my units, and one Salvage hit my commander for 2. Broadside killed my Rifle Squad. Then Vex attacked me with her Heavy Rail Rifle (a 5-attack weapon she'd equipped the turn before), which was exactly lethal. I never counted her weapon as "damage on the board".
   - *Defeat screen:* "Your forces pull back to regroup. Adjust your deck in the Hangar and try again." + "Advisor: Vex's Warp and Blitz units get +1 Attack. Keep a Guardian ready and deal with Warp threats quickly." This repeats the tip from turn 1 word for word. It didn't mention the weapon or the Det-Bot, which are what actually killed me. "Adjust your deck" gave me no idea *what* to adjust. I guessed from the Guardian hint and swapped Cargo Hauler for Blast Barricade. No Cadet offer after this first loss.
   - *Did it teach me anything?* A little ("Guardians"), but not the real lesson (count her weapon and Det-Bot damage, and don't go face at low health).
2. **2-6 Captain Vex, Officer, try 2 (died on Vex's turn 11).**
   - *Why I think I lost:* A long grind against her 35 Health. Her Broadside cleared my board twice. Shakedown deleted my Heavy Trooper, then my Star Cruiser. Cannon Volley picked off my 2-Health units every turn. My Guardians (Shield Trooper, Barricade, Okonkwo's Marines) all died early, so at 4 Health I had nothing to block with. I still played my three biggest affordable units the turn before I died, and none could stop her.
   - *Defeat screen:* identical text and tip to try 1. Then a new line: "That's 2 losses in a row here. You may retry this one battle on Cadet (weaker enemies): launch 2-6 --easy". **That line was clear and welcome.** I took it straight away.
   - *Did it teach me anything?* Not much. Getting the same advice twice felt like the game wasn't listening.

## Debrief answers
1. **How do you win, and three mechanics?** You knock the other commander's Health down to zero. (1) You spend Energy to put soldiers and ships down, and you get one more Energy each turn. Most can't attack until next turn, but "Warp" ones can attack straight away. (2) "Guardian" units have to be attacked first, so they protect you. (3) "Salvage" means something happens when the unit dies, like the Det-Bot exploding on you. I also learned, without being told, that you get hurt when you run out of cards.
2. **Story so far?** The space-lane machines (Lamps) went dark 70 years ago. Now a signal from the middle of the galaxy, "the Choir", is waking them up and getting into people's heads. I graduated from an academy run by a sarcastic computer called HALCYON, and Admiral Okonkwo gave me an old ship. A pirate queen, Vex, let the Choir in and it took her over. Mox, her salvage man, helped me. Next stop is the Hive Nebula and the Swarm.
3. **Best / worst / most confusing?** Best: 1-5, when the "keep a Guardian out" tip saved me on 5 Health. Worst: the second Vex loss, with the same useless tip and my board wiped twice. Most confusing: beating HALCYON because it ran out of cards, and the "2/undefined gear" cards.
4. **Difficulty over the session?** Flat and easy for 11 missions; I was rarely in real danger without the enemy letting me off. Then a cliff at Vex. Cadet Vex was easier than most Sector 1 fights. Nothing in between.
5. **New cards?** Getting cards after every fight was nice, and the Admiral card was a highlight. Crates of three cards I didn't understand (gear, Cloak) weren't exciting. I picked whichever had the biggest number or a sentence I understood. My only Academy pack gave two duplicates I couldn't keep.
6. **Cutscenes and story?** They added to it, and they were short. HALCYON's jokes landed. The Vex log was good.
7. **Play again tomorrow?** Maybe. I want to see the Swarm. What would stop me: another wall like Vex where the game tells me the same thing every time I lose.
8. **To a friend in one sentence:** "It's easy and kind of fun for a long while, then the pirate boss flattens you, but it'll let you drop the difficulty for that one fight."

## Top 5 findings for the designers (most important first)

1. **The Officer AI repeatedly passed up winning attacks, so Lee's mistakes were never punished until the final boss.** Lee played face-only, biggest-card-first and never defended, yet won 11 of 11 non-boss battles first time. Specific skipped wins: 1-5 T8 (three Warp Strike Fighters, 6 damage, against my 5 Health; all sent into units); 2-1 T6–T8 (12 to 18 attack on board against my 6 to 8 Health, mostly no attacks on me); 2-4 T6 and T8; 2-6 try 1 T8 (8 attack against my 9 Health; Interceptor and Det-Bots sat still). Enemy commanders also hit big units with weapons and nearly killed themselves (2-2 Rourke to 1 Health; 2-5 Wing Leader to 1). This contradicts the in-game description "Reckless. Races for your commander whenever the fight looks close". Effect on a struggling player: no losses means no lessons, so the first real lesson arrives at the boss. **Major.**
2. **Difficulty cliff at 2-6 Captain Vex, and Cadet overcorrects.** After ten easy wins, Officer Vex (35 Health, Broadside, Shakedown, Cannon Volley, 5-attack Heavy Rail Rifle, Det-Bots) beat me twice. On the Cadet retry she had 26 Health, held 6 to 7 cards most turns, and I won on 24 Health. "Hopeless" to "trivial" with nothing in between. **Major.**
3. **The defeat screen doesn't explain the loss and repeats itself.** Both losses showed the same "Adjust your deck in the Hangar and try again" and the same turn-1 tip about Warp/Blitz +1 Attack. Neither named what killed me: Vex's weapon (gear) damage and Det-Bot Salvage. "Adjust your deck" is a dead end with no hint about what to change. On the plus side, the "2 losses in a row… retry this one battle on Cadet" offer is clear and well-worded, and it kept Lee playing. But it only appears after the second loss, so the first defeat screen offers nothing new. **Major.**
4. **Advisor tips are static and sometimes about cards that aren't on the board.** One tip stays up all battle ("This is the full exam…" for 12 turns in 1-6; "Boarding Crew has Blitz" for 11 turns in 2-1 before any Boarding Crew appeared; Mine Layer and Det-Bot tips in 2-3 before either was played). The one time a tip matched the moment (1-5: "Keep a Guardian out when your Health is low", shown when I *was* low) it directly saved the game. That's evidence that situational advice works and front-loaded advice gets ignored. **Major.**
5. **Several mechanics reach the player untaught or with broken text.** Fatigue (taking damage once your deck is empty) is never explained, yet it decided the Sector 1 final exam (HALCYON died to its own fatigue). Gear first appears in the 2-1 crate as "common gear 2/undefined gear" and is only explained in the 2-2 briefing. HALCYON silently removes cards from your deck during Sector 1. Nanite Repair can be spent at full Health with no warning, and an earlier tip nudges you to spend spare Energy on your power. **Minor to major** (the "undefined" text is a bug).

## Suggestions (opinion)
- Make the Officer AI take a win when it has one on the board, at least from mid Sector 1, so small mistakes cost a game earlier, when the stakes are low and the lessons are simple. Or deliberately script one "you'd have died here" moment in Sector 1 with a defeat screen that explains it.
- Defeat screen: name the top one or two damage sources ("Most of your damage came from Vex's weapon: 10" / "Det-Bot explosions hit you for 4"). Give one concrete deck suggestion ("You have 2 Guardians; try adding Shield Trooper"). Don't repeat the pre-battle tip word for word.
- Offer the Cadet retry after the first boss loss, or offer a middle option. Make Cadet Vex less passive so the win still feels earned.
- Advisor: trigger tips on board state (low Health with lots of enemy attack showing; a Warp unit just arrived; enemy weapon equipped; deck nearly empty). Only mention a card once it's on the board.
- Teach fatigue (Archive entry plus a tip when the deck drops below about 3 cards). Fix "undefined" in gear stats. Explain Gear before offering gear in a crate. Tell the player when HALCYON changes their deck.
- Warn or grey out healing at full Health.

## Harness issues (text interface, not the game)
- **Index renumbering broke chained commands.** After a unit dies or a card is played, A#/E# numbers and hand numbers shift. In 2-1 T5 my "attack A3 E0" silently failed because my Sapper had died and A3 became A2, so my Interceptor never attacked. It caused several near-misplays elsewhere. A real touch screen doesn't have this problem.
- Combat log lines like "Heavy Trooper attacked Heavy Trooper. Heavy Trooper took 4 damage." and "Fleet Cadet attacked Fleet Cadet" don't say whose unit is whose.
- Keyword text is duplicated with raw markdown: "Guardian. **Guardian**", "Warp. **Warp**".
- "2/undefined gear" appears in `card`, crate and hand listings. It could be the harness failing to read gear durability, or game data; please check both.
- `deck autofill` printed the full deck list twice.
- `deck add void_plunderer` gave "No such card" because the ID is `plunderer`. Adding by display name in quotes worked. A player has no way to know card IDs except through `collection`.
- No Anchorage command in `help`, so 310 Alloy could never be spent. I can't tell whether that's a game dead end or a harness gap.
- The 13/15 deck warning shows on the first status screen even though 1-1 uses a fixed training deck.
- No opening-hand (mulligan) prompt in 1-1 to 1-3. It first appeared in 1-4, which matches its "NEW" tag there, so this is probably intended.
