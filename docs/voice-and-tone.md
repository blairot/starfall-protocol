# Starfall Protocol: voice and tone

This guide is for every piece of player-facing copy: cutscenes, the Archive, card lore and flavor, mission briefings, sector intros, advisor tips and end screens. It replaces the old "terse, mysterious" tone rules. The facts in `docs/story-bible.md` are still canon. Only the way we tell them has changed.

## The goal

A player who has never read a word of lore should finish every cutscene knowing three things: **what just happened, why it matters, and what we do next.** We tell a clear story about people we get to know. We do not speak in riddles.

## The blend

**Narration, Archive, card lore and flavor: plain-spoken military** (think *The Expanse*). Facts first. Short, complete sentences. Concrete and specific. A Fleet historian explaining things to a smart newcomer, with a dry sense of humour.

**Cutscenes and mission briefings: character-led** (think *Firefly*, *Halo* radio chatter). The crew carry the story in their own voices, with personality, banter and warmth. The narrator is used sparingly, mainly to set the scene and to sum up what the player now knows.

## Rules

1. **Explain every term the first time it appears**, in plain words. "Lamps, the giant alien machines that hold the lanes open." After that, use the term freely.
2. **Answer questions; don't pose them.** If a line hints at a mystery, the same scene (or the next one) should explain it. Never end on an unexplained tease.
3. **Every cutscene ends with a clear next step:** where we're going and why.
4. **Say what people want and feel.** Characters have plain motives: Vex wants the Belt, Okonkwo wants her officers alive, the Choir wants everyone to join it.
5. **Complete sentences.** No stacked fragments ("Not a ship. Not a fleet."), no twist-reversals ("It was never X. It was Y."), no "something out there" vagueness.
6. **Numbers only when they help.** "Over a million people died" beats "1.4 million". Drop clock times, exact counts and dates used only for atmosphere. Years are fine when they anchor the timeline.
7. **Humour is welcome**, especially from HALCYON, Mox and soldiers. Keep it dry and in character. No jokes in a death scene.
8. **No em dashes** in lore or dialogue. Use commas, colons, full stops or parentheses.
9. **Voiced lines are read aloud by a text-to-speech voice.** Write numbers and years as words ("twenty-two nineteen", "seventy years"). Avoid abbreviations, symbols and anything hard to pronounce. Keep each line under about 30 words.

## Avoid

- "Nobody knows…", "never said why", "something is coming", "whatever it is"
- Riddles and prophecy-speak: "the song remembers", "nothing that sings with us is lost" (unless a character is clearly quoting the Choir, and the scene explains what it means)
- Ominous one-word paragraphs and dramatic ellipses
- Lists of three for rhythm ("the machines, the Swarm and the psionics are all listening")
- Repeating a fact the player already knows in the same scene

## Glossary (use these plain definitions on first mention)

| Term | Plain definition |
| --- | --- |
| The Reach | The roughly four hundred human worlds, moons and stations, linked by warp lanes. |
| Warp lanes | Shortcuts through space that let a ship cross light-years in days. Humans found them; nobody human built them. |
| Lamps | The giant alien machines, each about a kilometre across, that hold the warp lanes open. |
| The Lamplighters | Spacers' name for the long-gone aliens who built the Lamps. |
| The Starfall | 14 March 2219: every Lamp failed for nineteen hours. Ships mid-jump were thrown into open space and over a million people died. Burning ships fell on New Lagos. |
| Directive 17 / the Starfall Protocol | The Fleet's secret order after the Starfall: shut down every Lamp leading toward the galactic core, and keep the reason classified. The game is named after it. |
| The Fleet | The Colonial Fleet, the navy of the Colonial Compact (the human government, founded at Concord). |
| The Belt / Scrap Belt | A ring of Starfall wreckage around the dead Lamp 9, run by raiders, smugglers and salvagers. |
| The Swarm | A species of living weapons, descended from an alien organism called the Progenitor. Kept behind a Fleet quarantine in the Hive Nebula. |
| The Forge | Three foundry worlds run by machine minds. They built most of the Fleet's ships. Governed by Calculus Prime. |
| The Psionic Orders | Humans who can sense the warp lanes with their minds. They live in the Void Expanse, beyond the Forge. Led by Hierarch Nyra. |
| The Choir | A single vast mind at the centre of the galaxy, made of the Lamplighters who merged together to escape death. It calls out to people through the lanes and absorbs anyone who answers. |
| The Singularity | The object at the galactic core where the Choir lives. |
| Echoes | Copies of absorbed people that the Choir rebuilds in black glass and sends to fight. |
| HALCYON | The Fleet Academy's training AI. Dry, precise, secretly fond of its cadets. |
| Anchorage | An abandoned Fleet station in the Belt that the player restores. |

## What the player learns, and when

Mysteries are revealed on schedule, and each reveal is stated plainly when it lands.

| After | The player now clearly knows |
| --- | --- |
| Opening | Lanes, Lamps and the Starfall. A signal has started coming from the galactic core. The Forge has gone quiet, the Swarm is breeding fast, and the dead Lamp in the Belt has started humming. The Fleet needs officers, so the player is at the Academy. |
| Sector 1 (graduation) | The player passes. Okonkwo gives them the Meridian and sends them to the Belt to find out why Lamp 9 is humming. |
| Sector 2 (Vex) | The signal is a mind that calls itself the Choir. It offers people what they want most, and those who accept are taken over. It turned Vex. Its next target is the Swarm. |
| Sector 3 (Ixis) | The Choir took the Swarm's queen and was using the Swarm as an army. The trail now leads to the Forge, which has gone silent. |
| Sector 4 (Calculus) | Calculus Prime matched the Choir's signal to its own records of the Starfall: the Choir caused the Starfall. After it, the Fleet secretly shut down every Lamp toward the core (Directive 17) to cut the Choir off. Hierarch Nyra has been switching them back on. |
| Sector 5 (Nyra) | The whole truth: the Choir is the Lamplighters, merged into one mind at the Singularity. It absorbs every mind it can reach, and the Starfall was its first attempt to pull in humanity. Nyra believed it offered peace. The way to the core is open, and the Meridian goes to end it. |
| Finale | HALCYON gave itself up to help the player. The Choir is destroyed, the Singularity collapses into an ordinary star, and the Lamps are safe. |

## Examples

**Narration (opening).**
Humanity lives on about four hundred worlds, linked by warp lanes: shortcuts through space that let a ship cross light-years in days. We didn't build the lanes. We found them, along with the giant alien machines that hold them open. Spacers call those machines Lamps.

**Dialogue (Vex, dying).**
VEX: "You hear it too, don't you? It told me the Belt was mine. Funny thing: it says that to everyone."
MOX: "Meridian, Mox here. Vex is gone. Her logs say the voice calls itself the Choir, and it's headed for the Swarm next. Lucky us."

**Card flavor.** One plain line, in a character's voice or the Fleet's dry voice.
Nyra: "She was the first human to hear the Choir, at nine years old. She believes it is offering peace."

**Card lore.** Two to four sentences: what it is, who uses it, one specific human detail.
Fleet Cadets are second- and third-year Academy students sent into live service when the Fleet runs short, which is always. They carry standard carbines and a rulebook they haven't finished reading.
